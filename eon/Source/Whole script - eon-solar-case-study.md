# E.ON — Turning Paid Traffic Into Solar Leads

---

## Hero

**Turning paid traffic into solar leads.**

Redesigning a high-friction journey — turning existing traffic into qualified solar leads.

**Role** — Led design end-to-end, from research through testing, with a junior designer — partnering with PM, dev, and PO on strategy and delivery.

**+300%** increase in leads · Rolled out to the UK market

---

## 01 — The Challenge

**We had the traffic. Not the leads.**

E.ON was investing in paid traffic, but too few visitors converted — baseline lead conversion sat around 5.1%. People were researching solar, comparing options — but dropping off before finishing the calculator. We never got their information.

The opportunity wasn't more traffic. It was more value from the traffic we already had.

---

## 02 — Our Audience

**Interest was there. Confidence wasn't.**

The German market — homeowners aged 40+, 66.7% of our audience. Early in their solar journey, still hesitating on three fronts —

- Does it suit my home?
- First cost concerns
- Sustainability-minded, but unsure where to start

---

## 03 — The Journey

**A narrow window, lost at Calculate.**

Visit → Explore → **Calculate** → Consider → Apply → Purchase

Homeowners typically made a decision within 24 hours of their first visit — but over 85% dropped off at Calculate before ever getting there. A slow, heavy calculator was costing us the entire decision window.

---

## 04 — The Problem

**The calculator asked for too much — and customers couldn't answer it anyway.**

A useful estimate required detailed information about the home. The original five or so questions were built for the algorithm, not for the customer. Some dropped off. Others never started.

**Design question** — How do we get the data we need, without making the customer do the work?

The friction wasn't refusal — it was distance. Roof pitch, orientation, shading: none of it was something a customer could answer on the spot. They'd have to go find it — check a document, measure something, ask someone. That meant leaving the flow — and with a 24 hour decision window, leaving once was often enough to lose them for good.

---

## 05 — Design Decisions

**A simpler path to a smarter estimate.**

We use the customer's location to retrieve key home details upfront — fewer inputs, fewer unnecessary questions.

| Before — Manual input | After — We found your roof |
|---|---|
| Roof size? | Roof size ✓ |
| Roof inclination? | Roof inclination ✓ |
| Sunlight exposure? | Sunlight exposure ✓ |

With location authorization, Google Maps data resolves all three fields automatically. Coverage was built for Germany first — once the approach proved out, the UK team localized it for their own market.

Customers who decline to share their location can still enter the details manually — the estimate is only as accurate as what they provide.

Three of the five original fields were solved this way. The other two needed a different fix.

---

## 06 — Testing & Iteration

**Ask what people know, not what they don't.**

Households didn't know their exact house area — but they knew how many floors they had, so we asked for that instead and estimated area from it. The same logic applied to electricity use: households didn't know their annual usage — asked directly, they guessed. We switched to asking household size instead, and estimated usage from that, with the option to enter an exact number manually. With fewer numbers to hunt for, completion time dropped by more than 10 minutes on average — fewer people stalled out, and more of them finished.

> "It was so much easier than expected."
> — Test user, Germany
