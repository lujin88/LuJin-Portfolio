What changes when AI enters the process?

The tools generate options faster than any team could. The risk is speed without direction — more artefacts, less clarity.

Speed· Volume· Judgment· Craft

So the question isn't can AI design this? It's how a designer stays in the lead — using AI to widen the option space while strategy, taste, and systems thinking decide what's worth building.

One loop. Four moves.

Explore→ Generate→ Prototype→ Iterate

01 — Explore · Start with better questions

Research, challenge assumptions, connect signals, and explore beyond the obvious. AI compresses the reading, but the framing — which questions are worth asking — stays the designer's call.
ChatGPT · Claude · Gemini · Perplexity
02 — Generate · Make possibilities visible

Turn ideas into visuals, motion, and directions — making abstract thinking tangible early, while it's still cheap to change your mind.
Kling · Seedance · MiniMax · Jimeng
03 — Prototype · Make it real enough to learn

Turn promising directions into working experiences and see what holds up under real interaction, not just in a static frame.
Figma · Canva · Paico
04 — Iterate · Make. Test. Learn. Repeat.

Push ideas through scenarios and edge cases, then rethink what doesn't work. The loop tightens each pass until the direction earns its place.
Claude · Codex

Where I come in.

AI can produce a hundred directions in an afternoon. It can't tell you which one fits the product, the users, or the constraints you haven't written down yet.

So every pass asks: what can I generate, what can I reuse, and what actually needs a decision?

The goal

The goal isn't more output.

It's making every direction earn the next step.
