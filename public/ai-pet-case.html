    Built for someone who can’t read yet

    Four rules came before any screen. Every later decision was checked against them.

    One character. Twelve feelings.

    A child reads a face faster than any icon. Every loop starts from one character sheet with an identity lock, and he always lies down — so cuts between loops disappear.

    When you go out, he comes too

    The knob stays plugged in at home. Outside, on mobile data, the same dolphin with the same voice is a phone call: a face, a timer, a red button. He rings first at lunch and bedtime, eats dinner with you when you swipe up, and you can always cut in.

    Silence over a wrong voice

    The easy fallback was the phone’s built-in speech. I removed it: if the voice service fails, Diandian waits and tries again instead of sounding like a sat-nav. French words are the one deliberate exception.

    The parent side

    Behind the PIN, a parent builds the friend in three steps: one character sheet, twelve motions made from it, then a one-time code that sends him to the child’s phone. The child never sees any of it.
