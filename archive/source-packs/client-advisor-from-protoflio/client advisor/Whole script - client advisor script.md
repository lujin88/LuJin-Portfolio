## 02 · The Mandate

**Finding where AI could make a difference.**

As AI moved deeper into banking, I saw an opportunity to rethink efficiency across Advisory and Sales.

**The key question**
Where could AI create the most meaningful impact?

Before optimising anything, I needed to understand where advisors were actually losing time — and why.

*Multiple tools. Siloed data. A hypothesis worth testing.*
*The opportunity: do things differently.*

---

## 03 · The Research

**Understanding the work.**
One need. A whole service behind it.

I set out to understand how advisors actually work — interviewing 17 Client Advisors primarily across Switzerland Wealth Management, with EMEA and APAC[^1] advisors included for comparison, to see how they moved across 10+ different tools, collaborated with others, and turned what they gathered into a decision for the client.

**The information existed. The advisor had to connect it.**

The friction wasn't access. It was stitching together 10+ disconnected tools — in the moment, under pressure.

> "I know the data exists — it's just hard to find."

> "The client calls, and I have minutes — sometimes seconds — to give them an answer. Finding the data isn't the hard part. Not knowing what I'm missing is."

---

## 04 · The Shift

**From information retrieval to decision support.**

The research reframed the question. Not *"How do we help advisors search faster?"* — but *"How does AI help advisors recognise what matters, understand why, and know what to do next?"*

This shifted AI's role — from finding information to supporting better decisions.

**Signal → Context → Act**
- **Signal** — What matters?
- **Context** — Why does it matter?
- **Act** — What happens next?

---

## 05 · Design Principles

**Turning the model into design decisions.**

The direction was already decided. It came with one constraint: it couldn't feel like another tool. Signal → Context → Act became the model for how it earned its place — three principles for every moment.

**01 · Surface what matters**
Not everything worth knowing deserves a place on screen. A signal earned its spot by urgency and stakes — a portfolio shift, a compliance flag — not by being one more thing to scroll past.

**02 · Explain with context**
A signal without a reason is just noise. Every one had to answer "why now," in the time it takes to glance — using the same research, market insights, and client notes the advisor would otherwise have to dig for.

**03 · Support judgement**
AI surfaces, it never decides. Every signal is a suggestion — the trade, the allocation, the response to the client stays entirely with the advisor.

---

## 06 · The Concept

**Designing for the moment of decision.**

Three paths were on the table: improve every existing tool individually, build a new unified dashboard, or design a surface layer above the existing landscape.

A new dashboard was the easier case to make internally — it was easier to scope, own, and fund as a standalone initiative. I argued for the surface layer instead: the goal wasn't a new product to own, it was fewer tools for advisors to juggle. Advisors were already working across 10+ systems — adding one more, or asking them to relearn a new one, would cost more than it saved.

That case won the stakeholder over.

Concretely, that meant a prioritised overview layered on the existing dashboard, expanding into full context on click, with next steps surfaced as recommendations — the way retail apps surface related picks.

One experience, unifying client context, portfolio, and research — without adding to the pile. 500+ client signals, one view.

*Concept and interaction model: mine. Visual design in collaboration with a UI/product designer.*

## 07 · The Outcome

**The outcome.**
A tested concept for more focused advisory.

I led concept testing with advisors — including the stakeholder who'd pushed for the dashboard — to see how the model held up against real workflows.

**Concept → Mockup → Test**

Average feedback from testing:
- 5–10 min projected savings per client on information gathering
- Up to 30 min projected savings on complex proposals

Feedback was consistent across EMEA and CH, where the concept was primarily scoped. APAC leaned toward speed over the signal-and-context flow — a nuance noted for future markets, outside this project's core focus.

**Reach**
500+ advisors across trials and target user base
