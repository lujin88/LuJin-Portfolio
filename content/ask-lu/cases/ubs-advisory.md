# Case: UBS — AI-powered advisor workflow (Investment Hub)

- Company: UBS
- Lu's role: Lead Service Designer, working with a UX researcher and a UX/UI designer
- Scope: service design: workflow mapping, information architecture, interaction flows and wireframes (not visual design)
- Designed for 500+ client advisors
- Status: concept. Feedback was positive and budget for the next phase was approved; not shipped yet.

## Problem
Client advisors use more than 10 bank tools a day: research, portfolio management, CRM, documents, messaging. Every switch means rebuilding context. Research showed advisors had built their own workarounds: notebooks, sticky notes, calendar reminders, and marking emails as unread to use Outlook as a to-do list. The information existed, but it was disconnected. The real bottleneck was how fast an advisor could get from a signal (e.g. a market move) to a decision they could defend.

## What Lu did
- Mapped end-to-end advisor workflows and identified the key friction points
- Contributed to advisor interviews about daily needs, priorities and opportunities for AI
- Facilitated a stakeholder workshop and made sure frontline advisors were in the room
- Translated the findings into information architecture, interaction flows and wireframes

## Key decision
The workshop weighed three directions: build a new dashboard, fix the existing tools one by one, or add a connecting layer. The team chose the connecting layer. As one advisor put it: "I already have ten tools. I don't need an eleventh."
Lu first proposed a new place on screen for the AI layer; stakeholders asked to reuse existing surfaces instead. The final concept embeds AI into existing widgets across the advisory workflow rather than adding a new screen.

## Concept
AI embedded into the advisors' existing workflow, rather than a separate new tool: helping them prioritise, surface relevant information, and respond to clients at the right moment. When a client calls, the advisor sees who the client is and what they may need.

## Outcome
Concept testing pointed to time savings from less searching, synthesising information and switching between systems: 5–10 minutes for a simple case, up to about 30 minutes for a complex case, which averages out to more than 10 minutes saved per client interaction. Advisors' main concern was delivery quality: whether AI-surfaced information would be good enough to use as is.
